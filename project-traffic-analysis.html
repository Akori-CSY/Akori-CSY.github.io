Summary

In Incident Response and Management lab classes, I analyse packet capture (PCAP) files provided by my lecturer in Wireshark, as practice for the module’s practical test. This is building my foundation in detecting suspicious network activity. I have also practised with Wireshark on my own. This module is ongoing.

How I did it

  1. Filter the capture

    Opened the lab PCAP file and applied display filters to focus on specific traffic, such as DHCP requests and failed connections.

  2. Follow a conversation

    Used Follow TCP Stream to read a full conversation between two hosts from start to finish.

  3. Look at the bigger picture

    Used the Statistics menu, including the I/O Graph, to see how traffic changed over time and spot unusual patterns.

Result and what I learnt

From the captures, I identified DHCP requests, the IP addresses of devices, the phone model a device was using, and failed connections.

I learnt how powerful Wireshark is. A single capture can reveal a surprising amount about a device and what it is doing on the network. That was eye-opening, and a little worrying, because it showed me how much someone could learn from captured traffic, and why monitoring and protecting it matters.
